Studio: a canvas change is applied to the picture in place, without loading the film again, so the film no longer jumps to the top left before it settles in the middle. Dragging the canvas's width or height in the inspector changes the picture as the mark moves. The picture no longer shows a light hairline along its right and bottom edges in the dark theme.
Studio's address keeps its key (`?key=…`), so an address copied from the address bar opens Studio in any browser on this computer, or in an agent's preview, without being signed out. The picture is clipped to its own box, so no line shows along its edges. The inspector is open whenever a project opens. A canvas change fits each picture to the new canvas on its own, as CapCut does, so a portrait photo on one clip no longer shrinks 16:9 footage on another. Dragging the canvas's width or height shows the size and shape on the picture and in the inspector as it moves (Custom unless it is a preset). A click on a page's empty part selects the whole page, so a page smaller than the canvas can be moved and sized on the picture. A canvas side typed or dragged stays from 16 to 8192 px and within 1:4 to 4:1. With a whole page in hand, a click on something in it selects that (a drag still moves the page). ⌘A (Ctrl+A) selects every layer on the picture; Shift-click and a drag on empty space select several, as before. Everything a click can select is outlined under the pointer: a page's layers, a whole page by its empty part, a video or a still, with a clip in hand too. A marquee around whole clips on the picture (by the part on the canvas) selects those clips, as Figma takes the frames it goes around; one inside a page selects its layers, as before. While a marquee is drawn, what letting go would select is outlined as it goes. Several clips selected have one frame around them all, and a drag on one of them moves them all, written in one step. The frame around several clips can be dragged anywhere inside it, empty space too, and its corners scale them all in proportion; its size shows below it. Several clips' frame also turns (its corner rings, Shift in 15° steps) and, for videos, stretches from its sides; the arrow keys move them all. Several layers selected show their shared looks in the inspector (type, appearance, adjustments, effects), and let go when the timeline is pressed or the playhead leaves their clip, as one layer does. A clip selected on the timeline away from the playhead has no frame on the picture until the playhead reaches it. A new mask has a hard edge (no feather). The inspector's menus (blend mode, font weight) and the export's range ends open Studio's own dark menu instead of the system's light list. The inspector follows Figma's: several things' X, Y, W and H are their bounds, typed to move or scale them together; Appearance has corner radius, one for all or each corner its own; Fill is a section of its own (a line's color, a box's background, with + and −); Stroke is too, drawn as an outline that adds nothing to the box, inside, centered or outside the edge; Effects add an inner shadow, a layer blur and a background blur beside the drop shadow and faded edges; type can be underlined or struck through. Under a mask, a crop or faded edges, which cut away what is past the edge, the stroke offers Inside only (a stroke already placed otherwise shows where it is, with a note). Setting one corner keeps the others; removing a fill leaves the layer see-through (not the page's own fill back) and still selectable; an SVG shape's fill can be set; an image's Fit button fits it. A clip that plays all of its file shows its in point grayed out, with a note to shorten it first. The Mask button on the picture's selection bar opens a menu of shapes (the one on now checked): the one picked is put on the clip, its handles on the picture. A clip's fades sit right under its position and size in the inspector. A marquee can start on the dark around the picture, as on Figma's canvas, so what sits at its edge can be boxed whole; a click there lets go of what is in hand.
